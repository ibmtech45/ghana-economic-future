Leadership profile

About Dr. Cassiel Ato Forson

Dr. Cassiel Ato Baah Forson, born on 5 August 1978 in Ajumako-Bisease in the Central Region, is a Ghanaian economist, chartered accountant and tax practitioner. He has been the Member of Parliament for Ajumako-Enyan-Esiam since January 2009.

He served as Deputy Minister for Finance from 2013 to 2017 under President John Dramani Mahama, and as a member of the Economic Management Team. He later became Minority Leader in Parliament. On 22 January 2025 he became Minister for Finance in President Mahama’s second government.

His first degree in accounting is from South Bank University, London, and he holds a PhD from KNUST. The Ministry of Finance describes him as a chartered accountant and tax practitioner with more than 20 years of public and private sector experience.

Sources: Ministry of Finance · Office-holder list · GhanaWeb profile, 9 Jan 2025.

  • 2009: elected MP for Ajumako-Enyan-Esiam
  • 2013–2017: Deputy Minister for Finance
  • 2023: Minority Leader
  • 2025: Minister for Finance

The Finance Minister is responsible for the Ministry of Finance and presents the annual Budget Statement to Parliament. Under the Public Financial Management Act, this must be done no later than 15 November each year.

The Court of Appeal acquitted and discharged him in the ambulance procurement case on 30 July 2024, by a 2–1 majority. On 23 January 2025 the Attorney-General discontinued the state’s remaining proceedings and appeal. The Attorney-General has called the case a political witch-hunt. Critics have questioned the discontinuation of several cases. We record both positions. Source.

Leadership and Future Vision

Please read: this is the supporters’ aspiration, not an announced candidacy or an official NDC position. Any decision belongs to Dr. Ato Forson and to the party’s democratic processes.
  • Supporters point to his parliamentary service since 2009 and his years as Deputy Finance Minister.
  • They argue that presenting budgets and managing a recovery is relevant experience for national leadership.
  • They want policy debate on jobs, local production and fiscal discipline to shape any future leadership race.
  • Critics and opposition voices dispute the government’s account of the recovery and focus on cost of living and debt.
  • The NDC has not announced a flagbearer contest, and President Mahama is the sitting President.
